# Enterprise Orchestration Slide Content Design

## Intent

Prepare content for the senior-stakeholder follow-up to Slide 5 and a separate,
value-led market slide. The audience needs a clear internal-versus-procured
resource view, the budget status, the platform's intended value, and an honest
summary of existing market overlap. Position the longer-term platform as both
an evidence-grounded answer experience and a governed way to prepare changes
for review and write them to connected applications.

## Goals

- Keep Slide 5 focused on deployment inputs and the internal/procured split.
- Mark resource ownership, availability, and costs as pending IT/procurement
  validation; do not imply confirmed internal capacity or invent a budget.
- Flag the storage sizing conflict in the current slide: 250 GB in the summary
  versus 2 TB expandable SSD in the requirements table.
- Add one separate value-led market slide with a small competitor strip.
- Describe the work as domain context engineering and orchestration for
  evidence-grounded answers, not as training an LLM from scratch.
- Show an intended progression from connecting domain context, to answering
  with evidence, to preparing a source-system change for human review and
  approval before write-back.
- Use examples such as creating a requirement or modifying a change notice;
  label write-back as a future capability, not as currently available.
- Distinguish documented competitor capabilities from the proposed project's
  differentiation opportunity.

## Non-goals and anti-goals

- Do not create or edit a PowerPoint deck; provide slide-ready content only.
- Do not estimate costs or assign resources to internal/procured categories
  without confirmation.
- Do not claim that competitors cannot connect engineering systems, or that
  this project is uniquely capable, without verified comparative evidence.
- Do not claim model training or fine-tuning.
- Do not imply autonomous or unreviewed changes to enterprise source systems.
- Do not claim write-back is implemented or available today.

## Approaches

1. **Evolve the value-led market slide (selected):** lead with the
   engineering-data/process problem, then show a progression from connected
   context to evidence-grounded answers to future, human-approved write-back.
   Keep a concise strip naming relevant offerings. This keeps the deck compact
   and makes the broader product ambition clear.
2. **Add a separate future-capability slide:** gives create/update actions more
   room, but adds a slide and can separate the action vision from the product's
   central value story.
3. **Competitor comparison table:** gives a direct market comparison but risks
   crowding the slide and making unsupported feature-by-feature claims.

Slide 5 will present requirements as requested inputs and mark sourcing and
budget as pending. The 250 GB/2 TB discrepancy will remain explicit for
validation. The market-value slide will distinguish today's evidence-answer
positioning from the longer-term action capability; show an agent preparing
create/update work for user review and approval before changes are written to
source applications.

vendor action/write-back statements on the revised slide must be tied to a
source that explicitly documents the capability.
